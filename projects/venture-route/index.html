THE SIX-WEEK ROUTE

One question.
Six chances to learn.

Each week pairs a practical activity with a draft artifact. The sequence is a proposal; the final schedule and delivery format are not set.

  1. WEEK 01 · NOTICE

    Choose a useful problem

    Describe a specific person, situation, or task that could work better. Write down the assumption you most need to check.

    MAKEA clear problem statement
  2. WEEK 02 · ASK

    Listen to people

    Prepare open questions and explore how people handle the situation now. Look for patterns, not compliments.

    MAKEAn interview plan and evidence notes
  3. WEEK 03 · MAP

    Understand alternatives

    Map current workarounds and existing options. Decide which part of the problem is worth focusing on.

    MAKEAn alternatives map and revised hypothesis
  4. WEEK 04 · MAKE

    Build the smallest test

    Sketch, storyboard, or prototype one useful moment. Keep the test small enough to change quickly.

    MAKEA low-fidelity prototype
  5. WEEK 05 · LEARN

    Test and refine the value

    Show the prototype to people who fit the problem. Notice what they understand, try, question, or ignore.

    MAKEA test log and refined value proposition
  6. WEEK 06 · CHOOSE

    Decide what comes next

    Summarise the problem, what you tried, what you learned, and the next experiment you would run.

    MAKEA concise evidence-based next-step plan

i The route is a draft curriculum. Dates, schedule, time commitment, facilitators, and cohort details remain to be confirmed.

WHO IT COULD BE FOR

You don’t need a polished pitch to begin.

The concept is aimed at students at the question or early-idea stage who are willing to investigate a problem before building around it.

  • ✓You have a curiosity, recurring frustration, or question worth exploring.
  • ✓You’re open to hearing that your first assumption may be wrong.
  • ✓You want to make and test something small before making bigger promises.

A FEW WORKING PRINCIPLES

Make the evidence useful.

The method is simple: understand the context, try a small response, then let what happens inform the next move.

01 / PEOPLE FIRST

Start with real context.

Look at the task, the person, and the current workaround before settling on a solution.

02 / KEEP IT SMALL

Test one assumption.

A rough, focused prototype can reveal more than a polished idea that nobody has tried.

03 / LEARN HONESTLY

Change course when needed.

Record what did not work, explain what changed, and make the next experiment more useful.

CLEAR ANSWERS

What’s real.
What’s still a draft.

Venture Route is a concept page. Here is what it does—and does not—represent.

Is Venture Route running now?

No. This is an independent programme concept. There is no confirmed cohort, schedule, application, or operating team.

Who is the proposed programme for?

Students exploring an early-stage problem or idea. Specific eligibility and cohort criteria would need to be decided before a real pilot.

Do I need an idea before starting?

No. The proposed route begins with a question or friction point; the work is to understand the problem before choosing a response.

How much time does it take, and what does it cost?

Those details have not been decided. The length is a proposed six weeks, but the schedule, weekly time commitment, delivery, and cost are all to be confirmed.

Can I apply through this page?

No. The interest form is a local interaction preview only. Nothing entered is transmitted or saved, and it does not register you for anything.

Is it affiliated with a university?

No. Venture Route is an independent portfolio concept and is not affiliated with or endorsed by a university.

Was this website made with AI?

Yes. This website prototype was built with AI assistance. The programme is a general student venture-sprint concept, not an AI course.
